**DAO Proposal: Increase Governance Accessibility and Protect Contributor Privacy  
Proposal Title  
Remove Voting Whitelist Requirement and Remove Geographic Restrictions for General DAO Members**

Submitted By  
@rainbowmagician1  
0xA4811219b12CE85B377Cd8197b929e98d1C3CdE1

  
**Summary**
This proposal seeks to improve accessibility and participation within the Redbelly Community DAO by:  
Removing the whitelist requirement for participation in DAO voting.  
Removing geographic restrictions for General DAO Members.  
Maintaining all existing geographic limitations for High Council Members and General DAO Leadership.  
The proposal aims to lower barriers to participation, better protect contributor privacy, and further support the DAO's long-term goal of progressive decentralisation.

**Problem & Rationale**
The Redbelly Community DAO Constitution states that the DAO exists to steward growth, adoption, and decentralisation of the community while progressively decentralising governance as the contributor base matures. It also identifies Progressive Decentralisation and Fair Access, with Structured Onboarding as founding principles.  
The current governance framework also establishes geographic limitations for leadership positions to ensure decentralisation and geographic diversity.  
This proposal does not seek to modify those leadership protections.  
Instead, it proposes that these geographic limitations should apply only to High Council Members and General DAO Leadership, allowing General DAO Members to participate regardless of country of residence.  
Additionally, removing the voting whitelist would reduce onboarding friction and better respect contributor privacy by avoiding unnecessary disclosure of personal information for members who simply wish to participate in governance.  
This proposal is intended to:  
Lower barriers to governance participation.  
Encourage wider community engagement.  
Improve contributor privacy.  
Preserve decentralisation safeguards where they matter most—leadership positions.  
Note: The uploaded governance documents do not explicitly document a voting whitelist requirement. If the whitelist exists as an operational or Snapshot configuration rather than a constitutional rule, that implementation should be referenced during governance discussion.
